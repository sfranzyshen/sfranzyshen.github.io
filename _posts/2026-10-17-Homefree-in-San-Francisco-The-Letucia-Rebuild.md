*This was my home for a little while ... 😁*

## A home on wheels

I lived in this van for the first half of a full rebuild of **Letucia**, a Burning Man art car we were working on over on **Treasure Island**.

I was living in **San Francisco, around Golden Gate and Webster**, during that period. Later, I spent time at the **North Beach Hotel**, going back and forth between staying there and living in the van while the rebuild continued.

It wasn't a conventional way of living ... but I loved that time in my life.

## The city and the people

I loved North Beach. I loved being able to spend time in that part of San Francisco ... but really, I loved **all of the city**.

The work on Letucia was part of the adventure, but so were the experiences, the people, and the friendships that came out of those days.

There are stories from that period that could fill volumes of a book someday. And some of the friendships I made will last a lifetime.

**I wouldn't trade that chapter of my life for any amount of money.**

If I could find a way to live an alternative life in San Francisco again, I would.

## Homeless ... or homefree?

Yes, I was technically homeless during part of that time.

But back then, I preferred to call it **“homefree.”** 🙂

I had a home ... it just happened to have wheels.

That word captured how I felt about the life I was living then. It was an adventure I remember with affection, and one I chose to embrace.

My later experiences navigating homelessness in Chico have been very different. Both are part of my story, and I don't want to pretend that every experience of living without conventional housing feels the same.

## Every person has a story

And that's really what I want people to understand when they look at this picture.

**Every person experiencing homelessness has a story about how they got there.** You can't know that story simply by looking at where they sleep, what they own, or how they live.

People are quick to assume drugs or mental illness must be involved. Sometimes those are part of someone's circumstances. Sometimes they're not.

Sometimes it's poverty, the loss of a home, a relationship, or a string of events that didn't go the way somebody hoped.

And sometimes ... **it's self-exploration.** A desire to travel, meet people, discover something about yourself, and experience a different way of living.

I became a father very young. Before I'd even finished high school, I had to become a provider and work my ass off. I took that responsibility seriously, but it meant growing up pretty fast.

I'd already had some adventures early in life, and those experiences exposed me to people, cultures, and worlds very different from the one I'd grown up in.

**Travel has always been in my blood.**

Somebody once told me my last name roughly translates to **“French traveler.”** Who knows how true that is ... I certainly haven't verified it. But I've always liked the thought. 😁

I've spent much of my life wanting to see what's around the next corner ... and sometimes the road itself has felt more like home than a fixed address.

## Before you decide someone needs fixing ...

I think one of the biggest mistakes we make is assuming that everyone living outside conventional housing has the same problem ... and therefore needs the same solution.

**Not everyone who is unhoused needs to be rescued from the life they're living.**

Some people desperately want housing and can't get it. Some need medical care, treatment, protection, or support. Some need only a little assistance to get through a rough patch. And some are exploring a way of living that doesn't fit neatly into everybody else's expectations.

That doesn't mean we ignore people or leave them without options.

It means we **listen before we intervene**.

Offer help. Make housing available and affordable. Keep people safe. But don't assume someone needs to be controlled, corrected, or forced into a program just because their life looks different from yours.

**Rules and restrictions can be reasonable and moderate**, designed around actual safety needs rather than making every person conform to one rigid version of how they should live.

The lower the unnecessary barriers, the more room people have to make decisions about their own lives.

## More than an old van

When I look at this photograph, I don't just see an old van.

I see **a chapter of my life that I loved** ... a city I still love ... and friendships that no amount of money could replace.

And I remember that from the outside, somebody might have looked at me and seen only another homeless man.

They would have missed almost the entire story.

**Before we decide what's wrong with someone ... maybe we should ask them about their journey.**
