**To:** California Attorney General's Housing Justice Team  
**Regarding:** City of Chico, California  
**From:** Scott Franzyshen, Chico, California

---

## Request for Review

I am writing to request a review of the **City of Chico's implementation of its affordable-housing obligations** and its policies concerning people experiencing homelessness.

My concern is not simply that Chico has a homelessness problem.

My concern is that, over a period of years, **substantial public resources have been devoted to managing homelessness while there appears to have been comparatively little measurable progress toward creating the permanent affordable housing necessary to end homelessness.**

The City has spent public resources on:

- emergency shelter;
- temporary shelter facilities;
- encampment removal;
- temporary fencing of public property;
- cleanup operations;
- enforcement activities;
- temporary services; and
- other responses associated with managing homelessness.

Many of these activities may serve legitimate immediate purposes.

**However, they do not substitute for permanent housing.**

---

## A Revolving-Door System

The result appears to be a **revolving-door system**.

People experiencing homelessness are moved from encampments into shelters, temporary programs, or other short-term arrangements and then continue waiting for housing that remains unavailable.

> **Public money continues to circulate through the system while the underlying shortage of deeply affordable housing remains.**

I am asking the Attorney General's Office to examine whether the City of Chico is meeting not merely the procedural requirements of California housing law, but also the **implementation requirements contained in its adopted Housing Element and related state housing requirements.**

---

# Areas I Am Requesting the State to Review

## 1. Implementation of Chico's Housing Element

Has the City completed the specific:

- programs;
- deadlines;
- zoning changes;
- incentives;
- affordable-housing actions; and
- other commitments

contained in its adopted Housing Element?

---

## 2. Production of Affordable and Lower-Income Housing

What housing has actually been **permitted and completed** for:

- extremely low-income households;
- very low-income households;
- low-income households; and
- moderate-income households

compared with Chico's stated housing needs and RHNA allocation?

---

## 3. Use of Public Housing and Homelessness Funds

How much local, state, and federal funding received or administered by the City has been directed toward:

### Permanent solutions

- permanent affordable housing;
- deeply affordable housing;
- supportive housing;
- rehabilitation;
- acquisition;
- housing development; and
- permanent exits from homelessness?

### Temporary or management-oriented responses

- emergency shelter;
- temporary facilities;
- homelessness management;
- enforcement;
- encampment cleanup;
- fencing;
- administration; and
- related activities?

> **I believe the public deserves to know how much is being spent managing homelessness compared with actually creating housing.**

---

## 4. Outcomes From Homelessness Expenditures

Are publicly funded programs being evaluated based upon **permanent housing outcomes**, or primarily by the number of people:

- contacted;
- sheltered;
- served;
- enrolled;
- assessed; or
- temporarily accommodated?

The number of people who pass through a program is not necessarily the same thing as the number of people who successfully leave homelessness.

---

## 5. Removal of Barriers to Affordable Housing

Has the City taken all actions promised in its Housing Element to:

- remove governmental constraints;
- facilitate affordable development;
- identify suitable sites;
- encourage development of lower-income housing; and
- make those opportunities realistically feasible?

---

## 6. Housing Opportunities for Marginalized and Extremely Low-Income Residents

Are City policies and expenditures providing meaningful access to permanent housing for:

- people with little or no income;
- people with disabilities;
- seniors;
- people leaving homelessness; and
- others who cannot afford Chico's prevailing market rents?

---

## 7. Economic Opportunity

Where federal, state, or local programs administered by the City contain requirements or commitments concerning economic opportunity for low-income or marginalized residents, I ask that the State examine whether those commitments are actually being implemented.

This should include whether meaningful:

- employment opportunities;
- job training;
- pathways toward self-sufficiency; and
- economic participation

are being created for the populations these programs are intended to serve.

---

# Concentration of Public Resources and Barriers to Alternative Providers

I am also concerned about the **concentration of City support, access, contracts, and permissions among a relatively small group of established homeless-service providers.**

Over time, a few organizations appear to receive a substantial share of the public resources devoted to homelessness in Chico, including:

- funding;
- contracts;
- referrals;
- facilities;
- access to City resources; and
- official participation in the City's shelter system.

My concern is **not** that these organizations should receive no public support.

My concern is whether the City's processes provide **fair and meaningful opportunities for other nonprofit, community-based, faith-based, volunteer, or privately funded organizations that may wish to pursue different approaches.**

---

## Sanctioned Camping and Alternative Approaches

This concern is particularly significant regarding **sanctioned camping and other low-cost alternatives.**

The City has permitted or operated designated sheltering and camping arrangements under particular circumstances.

At the same time, community groups seeking to provide privately funded alternatives may face:

- regulatory barriers;
- permitting barriers;
- land-use restrictions;
- administrative barriers; or
- other obstacles

that effectively prevent them from offering similar options.

This raises an important question:

> **If the City can allow one organization or program to operate a campsite or alternative sheltering arrangement, why are other organizations not given the same opportunity when they are willing to meet legitimate health, safety, and operational requirements?**

---

# Questions That Deserve Independent Review

I respectfully request that the State examine the following:

1. **What criteria does the City use to determine which organizations may operate shelters, sanctioned campsites, transitional housing, or other facilities serving people experiencing homelessness?**

2. **Are those criteria written, objective, publicly available, and applied consistently?**

3. **Are City contracts and homelessness-related funding opportunities awarded through open and competitive processes?**

4. **How much City-controlled homelessness funding has gone to each provider during the past five years?**

5. **What percentage of those resources has gone to the same small group of organizations year after year?**

6. **Have other organizations applied for funding, permits, land-use approvals, operating agreements, or permission to provide alternative sheltering arrangements and been denied?**

7. **If privately funded organizations have proposed lawful sanctioned camping or other alternatives, what specific laws, zoning requirements, health and safety standards, or City policies prevented those proposals from proceeding?**

8. **Are those same standards being applied equally to programs sponsored, funded, operated, or selected by the City?**

9. **Does the City's existing system unnecessarily restrict competition, innovation, or alternative approaches to homelessness?**

10. **Does concentrating resources and authority among a small number of incumbent providers create incentives to maintain an emergency-service system rather than expand permanent housing alternatives?**

---

## Equal Standards and Equal Access

I am particularly concerned about situations in which **one organization is permitted to provide a particular type of shelter or campsite while other organizations appear unable to obtain equivalent permission**, even when those organizations propose to operate with private funding.

If there are legitimate:

- health;
- safety;
- zoning;
- insurance;
- environmental; or
- operational

reasons for treating those proposals differently, those reasons should be **transparent and consistently applied.**

If there are not, then the State should determine whether the City's practices are unnecessarily preventing additional organizations from contributing:

- shelter;
- housing;
- employment;
- transitional options; or
- other innovative solutions.

---

# Request for Review of Contracting and Funding Practices

I therefore request that the State examine Chico's:

- contracting procedures;
- grant awards;
- permitting decisions;
- land-use approvals;
- memoranda of understanding;
- requests for proposals;
- provider-selection processes; and
- other arrangements involving homelessness-service providers.

The purpose of this review should be to determine whether public resources and opportunities are being distributed through **fair, transparent, competitive, and lawful processes.**

---

# Managing Homelessness Versus Ending Homelessness

I recognize that emergency shelter, outreach, sanitation, and public-safety expenditures can be necessary.

My concern is the **balance of those expenditures** and whether temporary interventions have effectively become the City's long-term response.

> **A system that repeatedly shelters, relocates, processes, and serves the same population without producing enough permanent housing risks institutionalizing homelessness rather than resolving it.**

The question is not whether people experiencing homelessness should receive services.

They absolutely should.

The question is whether our public system has become better at **managing homelessness than ending it.**

---

# Request for State Investigation

I am therefore requesting that the **California Department of Justice**, working with the **California Department of Housing and Community Development** where appropriate, review:

- Chico's Housing Element implementation;
- Annual Progress Reports;
- affordable-housing production;
- homelessness expenditures;
- relevant funding records;
- contracting and provider-selection practices;
- permitting and land-use decisions affecting alternative providers; and
- compliance with applicable state housing laws.

I am also asking that the State determine whether Chico's reported compliance accurately reflects what has actually been **implemented and accomplished on the ground.**

---

# Supporting Documentation

I can provide supporting documentation, including:

- City financial records;
- Housing Element documents;
- Annual Progress Reports;
- Annual Action Plans;
- CAPER reports;
- public contracts;
- City payment records;
- homelessness-program information;
- funding records;
- public meeting records; and
- other public documents.

This complaint is **not intended to argue that every dollar spent on shelter or homelessness services is improper.**

It is a request for accountability regarding whether Chico's housing policies and expenditures are producing the permanent affordable-housing outcomes California's housing laws are intended to encourage.

---

## Closing

I believe Chico's residents deserve a clear accounting of:

> **What was promised?**  
> **What was funded?**  
> **What was actually built?**  
> **Who received the money?**  
> **What outcomes were achieved?**  
> **And why are alternative solutions sometimes prevented from even being attempted?**

Thank you for reviewing this matter.

**Sincerely,**

**Scott Franzyshen**  
Chico, California
