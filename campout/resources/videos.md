<h1>Video Discussion Guide</h1>
<p><strong>Recommended, not required:</strong> These video selections are prompts for open discussion, not a test of agreement. <a href="{{ '/campout/why-were-here/' | relative_url }}">Read why we're here</a>.</p>
<div class="resource-policy" style="background:#eff7f8;border-left:4px solid #006474;padding:.85rem 1rem;margin:1rem 0;">
<strong>Recommended Resources ... Not Required</strong>
<p>Our educational materials reflect a <strong>“shelter first” and lowest-barrier approach</strong> to addressing homelessness. They are recommended resources, not required material. If your group chooses not to use them, your participation is still encouraged.</p>
<p><strong>Awareness doesn't take sides ... all voices and perspectives are encouraged.</strong> However, Campout for Compassion will not provide or recommend “treatment first” materials.</p>

<p class="intro">Choose one video or an excerpt that fits your group. These are optional conversation starters ... not a required lesson plan, and not a substitute for a local conversation with someone who has experienced homelessness.</p>
<h2>Choosing videos by age</h2>
<p><strong>Ages 6–10:</strong> The drawing, home-safety, and kindness activities in the <a href="{{ '/campout/resources/activities/' | relative_url }}">activity guide</a> are a better starting point. No video is automatically recommended for young children.</p>
<p><strong>Ages 11–13:</strong> An adult-previewed, non-graphic excerpt of “Can You Tell Who is HOMELESS?” may be suitable. Use simple, respectful questions.</p>
<p><strong>Ages 14–17:</strong> Consider language, teen and family housing topics, or a pre-screened firsthand story. Offer a non-video option.</p>
<p><strong>Adults:</strong> Explore housing affordability, dignity, neighborhood concerns, services, and outcomes. Allow room for disagreement.</p>
<p class="notice"><strong>Please preview before showing:</strong> We verified the publisher pages and titles, but have not viewed every video in full or verified age ratings, run times, caption availability, or all potentially upsetting scenes. The short descriptions below identify discussion themes rather than summarizing a verified complete transcript. Facilitators must screen the videos first. Never require a participant to disclose their own experience. Some people in the room may have experienced housing insecurity.</p>
<h2>Easy discussion format</h2>
<p><strong>Before (5 minutes):</strong> Ask what people already think or wonder. Agree on respectful language. <strong>Watch (5–15 minutes):</strong> Show one selection or excerpt. <strong>After (10–15 minutes):</strong> What surprised you? What do you question? What might you do differently as a neighbor?</p>
<h2>Language, stereotypes, and human dignity</h2><p>Use these to open a discussion about assumptions, labels, and the values we share.</p>
<div class="video-card"><h3><a href="https://www.orgcode.com/youtube-videos/v/npm7wshpjrcjgdlp5apd5janx3jmrw" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Can You Tell Who is HOMELESS?</a></h3><p><strong>Why show it:</strong> A conversation starter about the assumptions we make from clothing, appearance, and first impressions.</p><p class="meta"><strong>OrgCode</strong> · Suggested group: Ages 11–13+ with adult preview</p><p><strong>Discuss:</strong> What assumptions do we make based on appearance?</p></div>
<div class="video-card"><h3><a href="https://www.orgcode.com/youtube-videos/v/wsahzesjchlhj8r373zjgm7ft5y5cy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Why PERSON-FIRST Language is Important.</a></h3><p><strong>Why show it:</strong> Introduces the idea of describing someone as a person rather than reducing them to a housing status.</p><p class="meta"><strong>OrgCode</strong> · Suggested group: Teens 14+ and adults</p><p><strong>Discuss:</strong> How can language describe someone's situation without defining the person?</p></div>
<div class="video-card"><h3><a href="https://www.orgcode.com/youtube-videos/v/29rnf5pc36ammyk3amwljabjep2h9e" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">The Missing Piece in Ending Homelessness: Shared Values</a></h3><p><strong>Why show it:</strong> Opens discussion about shared community values among people who disagree about homelessness policy.</p><p class="meta"><strong>OrgCode</strong> · Suggested group: Adults and mixed community groups</p><p><strong>Discuss:</strong> What do we have in common even when we disagree about solutions?</p></div>
<h2>Families, young people, and housing costs</h2><p>These options introduce forms of homelessness that may not look like an encampment.</p>
<div class="video-card"><h3><a href="https://www.orgcode.com/youtube-videos/v/jxj9yyrw8cs6f7mcfpgrr3lw2xwpn2" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Why So Many Teens Are Becoming Homeless</a></h3><p><strong>Why show it:</strong> Explores why young people may lose stable housing and what support they need to stay safe.</p><p class="meta"><strong>OrgCode</strong> · Suggested group: Teens 14+ and youth leaders</p><p><strong>Discuss:</strong> What support could help a young person keep a safe place to stay?</p></div>
<div class="video-card"><h3><a href="https://www.orgcode.com/youtube-videos/v/ez2gag84azet7lfkw8t5csranjlz6s" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">The Reality of Family Homelessness</a></h3><p><strong>Why show it:</strong> Introduces the forms of family homelessness that can be less visible than sleeping outdoors.</p><p class="meta"><strong>OrgCode</strong> · Suggested group: Older teens and adults</p><p><strong>Discuss:</strong> Why can family homelessness be difficult to recognize?</p></div>
<div class="video-card"><h3><a href="https://invisiblepeople.tv/videos/hidden-homeless-mother-arleen-family-hotel-crisis/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Arleen: A Mother's Fight for Stability in America's Hidden Homeless Crisis</a></h3><p><strong>Why show it:</strong> A first-person account of a mother facing housing instability and uncertainty while caring for her family.</p><p class="meta"><strong>Invisible People</strong> · Suggested group: Older teens and adults</p><p><strong>Discuss:</strong> What becomes more difficult for children when housing is unstable?</p></div>
<div class="video-card"><h3><a href="https://invisiblepeople.tv/videos/cheri-homeless-senior-st-louis/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Cheri - Homeless Senior Living in Hotel</a></h3><p><strong>Why show it:</strong> A senior's first-person account that opens conversation about income, shelter, and the cost of housing.</p><p class="meta"><strong>Invisible People</strong> · Suggested group: Adults and service groups</p><p><strong>Discuss:</strong> Why might benefits or employment still not cover housing costs?</p></div>
<div class="video-card"><h3><a href="https://www.orgcode.com/youtube-videos/v/h6c3tbmlalw47jfxshejesawwj95ra" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Why So Many Seniors Are Becoming Homeless</a></h3><p><strong>Why show it:</strong> Opens discussion about the pressures older adults face when rent and resources do not match.</p><p class="meta"><strong>OrgCode</strong> · Suggested group: Adults and church groups</p><p><strong>Discuss:</strong> What support helps older neighbors remain safely housed?</p></div>
<h2>Shelters, services, and meaningful choices</h2><p>These raise questions about whether an option can actually meet someone's needs.</p>
<div class="video-card"><h3><a href="https://www.orgcode.com/youtube-videos/v/pmsgl34l5cdll7t34mggws3yhnr9hl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Why Homeless Services Fail Without Choice</a></h3><p><strong>Why show it:</strong> Explores why services can fail when they do not account for people's actual circumstances and choices.</p><p class="meta"><strong>OrgCode</strong> · Suggested group: Adults and service organizations</p><p><strong>Discuss:</strong> What makes a service workable for one person but not another?</p></div>
<div class="video-card"><h3><a href="https://invisiblepeople.tv/videos/melissa-why-homeless-couples-choose-the-streets-over-shelter-in-seattle/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Melissa: Why Homeless Couples Choose the Streets Over Shelter in Seattle</a></h3><p><strong>Why show it:</strong> A first-person perspective on the complicated decision to use shelter when couples cannot remain together.</p><p class="meta"><strong>Invisible People</strong> · Suggested group: Ages 16+ and adults</p><p><strong>Discuss:</strong> What matters besides bed availability when considering shelter options?</p></div>
<div class="video-card"><h3><a href="https://www.orgcode.com/youtube-videos/v/92pcbc959676emy394wy6t3cb6fxas" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Housing: Human Right or Reward?</a></h3><p><strong>Why show it:</strong> Raises the question of whether safe housing should depend on meeting conditions or be a foundation for stability.</p><p class="meta"><strong>OrgCode</strong> · Suggested group: Adults and discussion groups</p><p><strong>Discuss:</strong> What do people mean by compassion and accountability in housing?</p></div>
<h2>Neighborhood concerns and responses to encampments</h2><p>Best for adult civic conversations. These include debates about enforcement and potentially distressing displacement experiences.</p>
<div class="video-card"><h3><a href="https://www.orgcode.com/youtube-videos/v/hc7gt6d6kkkdsk8kgecmhw2k643mt8" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Homeless ENCAMPMENT Response 101</a></h3><p><strong>Why show it:</strong> Introduces approaches and outcome measures for community responses to outdoor encampments.</p><p class="meta"><strong>OrgCode</strong> · Suggested group: Adults and civic groups</p><p><strong>Discuss:</strong> How should success be measured for an encampment response?</p></div>
<div class="video-card"><h3><a href="https://invisiblepeople.tv/videos/skid-row-homeless-sweeps-make-homelessness-worse/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Skid Row Homeless Sweeps Make Homelessness Worse</a></h3><p><strong>Why show it:</strong> A firsthand reporting perspective on displacement, sweeps, and what happens after a camp is cleared.</p><p class="meta"><strong>Invisible People</strong> · Suggested group: Adults and ages 16+ with care</p><p><strong>Discuss:</strong> What happens when people are moved but not housed?</p></div>
<div class="video-card"><h3><a href="https://www.orgcode.com/youtube-videos/v/jlaljxrcd5ht4zrw2smhle7p4pcw4g" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Don't Want Homelessness Services in Your Backyard? Front yard it is.</a></h3><p><strong>Why show it:</strong> Examines neighborhood objections and how community concerns may be discussed constructively.</p><p class="meta"><strong>OrgCode</strong> · Suggested group: Adults and neighborhood groups</p><p><strong>Discuss:</strong> How do we hear neighborhood concerns without dehumanizing anyone?</p></div>
<div class="video-card"><h3><a href="https://invisiblepeople.tv/videos/kim-from-freezing-in-a-tent-to-the-safety-of-a-home/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Kim: From Freezing in a Tent to the Safety of a Home</a></h3><p><strong>Why show it:</strong> A story of moving from a tent toward stable housing, useful for discussing what meaningful progress looks like.</p><p class="meta"><strong>Invisible People</strong> · Suggested group: Older teens and adults</p><p><strong>Discuss:</strong> What would lasting success look like for someone facing repeated displacement?</p></div>
