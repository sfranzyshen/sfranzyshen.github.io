<h1>Age-Appropriate Educational Activities</h1>
<div class="resource-policy" style="background:#eff7f8;border-left:4px solid #006474;padding:.85rem 1rem;margin:1rem 0;">
<strong>Recommended Resources ... Not Required</strong>
<p>Our educational materials reflect a <strong>“shelter first” and lowest-barrier approach</strong> to addressing homelessness. They are recommended resources, not required material. If your group chooses not to use them, your participation is still encouraged.</p>
<p><strong>Awareness doesn't take sides ... all voices and perspectives are encouraged.</strong> However, Campout for Compassion will not provide or recommend “treatment first” materials.</p>

<p>CHICO CAMPOUT FOR COMPASSION • PRINTABLE ACTIVITIES</p>
<h2>SHEET A • AGES 6–10 • 25 MINUTES</h2>
<p>Elementary: What makes a home? Purpose: Help children understand that people need more than walls and a roof ... they need safety, stability, connection, and dignity.</p>
<p>Facilitator opening: “People live in different kinds of homes. Some families have to move often, and some people do not have a stable place to stay. No one should be teased or judged because of where they live.”</p>
<p>1. Draw a place that helps someone feel safe (10 minutes) Inside the box, draw or write three things that help a person feel safe and cared for.</p>
<p>2. Talk together (10 minutes) • Which of these things does everyone need?</p>
<p>• How would you feel if you had to move and did not know where you would sleep?</p>
<p>• What can you do to make someone feel welcome and respected?</p>
<p>3. One kind action (5 minutes) This week I can show kindness by: ________________________________________________</p>
<p>Host note: Keep examples gentle, do not ask about children’s personal housing situations, and allow anyone to skip sharing.</p>

<h2>SHEET B • AGES 11–13 • 30–35 MINUTES</h2>
<p>Middle grades: Everyday needs map Purpose: Understand how stable housing makes ordinary routines easier ... and how communities can reduce barriers.</p>
<p>1. Choose three everyday needs Circle three: Sleep | Toilets | Food | Phone charging | Laundry | Transportation | Shower | Storage | School supplies | Quiet place to study</p>
<p>2. Fill in the chart (15 minutes)</p>
<p>NEED When a person has a stable home ... What may become difficult without one?</p>
<p>________________ ________________________ ________________________</p>
<p>________________________ ________________________</p>
<p>________________ ________________________ ________________________</p>
<p>________________________ ________________________</p>
<p>________________ ________________________ ________________________</p>
<p>________________________ ________________________</p>
<p>3. Name a community response (10 minutes) Pick one difficulty. What organization, public facility, friend, service, or housing option might reduce it?</p>
<p>Discuss: Why might it be difficult to keep a job or attend school when basic routines become unpredictable? Do not assume a participant’s housing situation.</p>

<h2>SHEET C • AGES 14–17 • 35–45 MINUTES</h2>
<p>Teens: Decisions without easy answers Purpose: Learn to recognize real-world constraints and ask what practical options are missing. The fictional situations are for analysis ... not role-play.</p>
<p>Read one fictional situation per small group (10 minutes)</p>
<p>SITUATION 1 • Work hours</p>
<p>A retail worker ends an evening shift after a shelter’s latest admission time. The worker has income but cannot find a room within budget.</p>
<p>SITUATION 2 • Keeping family together</p>
<p>A parent is offered a temporary arrangement that cannot accommodate their child or meet the family’s transportation needs.</p>
<p>SITUATION 3 • School stability</p>
<p>A student is staying temporarily with relatives. The commute and uncertainty make attendance and homework harder.</p>
<p>SITUATION 4 • Belongings and documents</p>
<p>An adult has no safe storage for work clothes, identification, or medication and must travel to appointments across town.</p>
<p>Questions (20 minutes) • Which needs are immediate? Which problems are caused by a missing resource rather than a poor</p>
<p>personal decision?</p>
<p>• What options does the person realistically have? What key information is missing?</p>
<p>• What would make this situation easier ... housing, schedule flexibility, safe storage, transit, services, or something else?</p>
<p>Write one specific improvement for Chico (10 minutes)</p>
<p>Facilitator rule: The goal is to notice limited choices, not ask teenagers to guess what homelessness “feels like” or rank who deserves help.</p>
<p>CHICO CAMPOUT FOR COMPASSION • PRINTABLE ACTIVITIES</p>
<h2>SHEET D • ADULTS / MIXED GROUPS • 45 MINUTES</h2>
<p>Adults: Three perspectives, one community Purpose: Practice listening without requiring agreement on every policy.</p>
<p>Read three viewpoints, then discuss</p>
<p>Neighbor: “I’m worried about safety, trash, and how usable our neighborhood parks feel.” Person without housing: “I need somewhere safe to sleep and keep my things ... and I don’t always have a realistic alternative.” Service provider: “We want to help, but programs have limits and we cannot promise a housing placement to everyone right away.”</p>
<p>1. What does each person want? Avoid telling them what they “really” mean. (10 minutes)</p>
<p>2. Identify two shared goals. (10 minutes)</p>
<p>3. List two questions that need facts, not assumptions. (10 minutes)</p>
<p>4. Identify one respectful next step. (10 minutes)</p>
<p>Facilitator: Acknowledge distinct perspectives, but do not require participants to agree with a harmful generalization or abandon their own experience.</p>

<h2>SHEET E • AGES 11+ • 15–20 MINUTES</h2>
<p>Optional: Listening and language exercise Purpose: Notice the difference between a fact, an assumption, and a respectful question.</p>
<p>Discuss each sentence. What is observed? What is assumed? How might the speaker ask a better question?</p>
<p>Statement Observed fact or interpretation?</p>
<p>Better question to ask</p>
<p>“Everyone on the street refuses help.” ____________________ ____________________</p>
<p>________________________ ________________________</p>
<p>“Moving tents means fewer people are homeless.”</p>
<p>____________________ ____________________</p>
<p>________________________ ________________________</p>
<p>“People in shelters have no problems.” ____________________ ____________________</p>
<p>________________________ ________________________</p>
<p>“Our neighborhood feels less safe.” ____________________ ____________________</p>
<p>________________________ ________________________</p>
<p>Debrief: What words could help us talk about a difficult community issue while still recognizing the dignity of all neighbors?</p>

<h2>SHEET F • ANY AGE (ADAPT AS NEEDED)</h2>
<p>Reflection: What changed for you? Complete individually, with a partner, or as a closing circle. Sharing is optional.</p>
<p>One thing I learned today ...</p>
<p>One assumption I want to reconsider ...</p>
<p>One question I still have ...</p>
<p>One way I could treat a neighbor with more dignity ...</p>
<p>For overnight participants: What could you stop doing tomorrow that someone without a safe home cannot simply stop? (Keep the focus on differences, not equivalence.)</p>
<p>End with this: “Our night was temporary. What we learned can stay with us.”</p>
