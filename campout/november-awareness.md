# November 2026 ... A Month to Learn, Listen, and Help

**One Night. Many Locations. One Community.** Our one-night **Chico Campout for Compassion is Saturday, November 14, 2026**, but awareness and compassionate action need not begin or end with a single night.

November is also **National Homeless Youth Awareness Month** (recognized by Congress beginning in 2007) and **California Youth Homelessness Outreach, Prevention, and Education (HOPE) Month** (recognized for November 2026 by California SCR 197). **National Hunger and Homelessness Awareness Week is November 15–21, 2026** according to [HHWeek.org](https://hhweek.org/). These independent national and state observances provide a wider context for our local conversations. We are **not claiming sponsorship or endorsement** from their organizers.

## Anyone can take part ... all month

You **do not have to camp out**, attend a meeting, donate, or spend money to participate. Read an article, share a verified resource, listen to someone with firsthand experience, have a family conversation, ask a school to discuss housing stability, or support a food program when you can.

### A simple November food and basic-needs drive

Food drives are **encouraged throughout November**, completely separate from event attendance. The Campout itself **does not collect funds or operate a central donation program**. Individuals, neighborhoods, churches, classrooms, clubs, and businesses can arrange donations **directly with an existing local organization**.

1. **Choose the recipient first.** Ask a local food pantry, shelter, community meal program, or mutual-aid group what it actually needs, when it accepts donations, and whether a drive would help.
2. **Collect only requested items.** Prioritize unopened, in-date, nonperishable, easy-open, ready-to-eat foods when a recipient requests them. Consider water, hygiene items, socks, or other supplies **only if requested**. Respect dietary, cultural, and accessibility needs.
3. **Make giving easy and optional.** Offer a labeled box at a workplace, church, class, neighborhood gathering, or your home, or donate straight to the program yourself. No Campout attendance is necessary; nobody should be pressured to give.
4. **Deliver directly and responsibly.** Coordinate sorting, transport, and delivery dates. Do not publicly announce someone else's premises as a drop-off site without approval. Do not leave food unattended.
5. **Share the lesson.** Tell participants who the recipient was (with permission), what need was identified, and what they learned. Avoid photographs or stories of recipients without informed consent.

**Local examples to contact independently (not announced Campout partners):**

- [Safe Space Chico ... donation guidance](https://www.safespacechico.org/donate) asks for unexpired, nonperishable, ready-to-eat, easily opened food and offers guidance about other needed items.
- [Community Action Agency of Butte County ... donation information](https://www.buttecaa.com/donate/) describes food-bank volunteer opportunities and separately lists accepted household and food donations for Esplanade House.
- [Chico Food Project](https://www.chicofoodproject.org/) organizes recurring community food collections. Review its pickup calendar before planning a November drive; its published 2026 pickup dates do **not** include November.

Contact the organization first, because needs, capacity, and delivery arrangements can change. Donating time, sharing a food-access resource, or simply helping a neighbor find a program also counts as taking action.

## Learning activities for November

**For families and younger children:** Talk about what makes a home safe and stable, how families may need help, and why food and housing are basic needs. Choose an age-appropriate book, drawing activity, or respectful conversation. Do not ask children to disclose their family housing situation.

**For teens, schools, and youth groups:** Use the [HETAC National Homeless Youth Awareness Month toolkit](https://www.hetac.org/hetac-resources/youth-awareness). It offers school activities, a calendar, books and films, videos, sample resolutions, and social media materials. HETAC suggests **November 10 as #WearGreenDay**. An optional class could identify a school support contact, create a resource poster, or organize a recipient-approved supplies drive.

**For adults, faith groups, clubs, and neighbors:** Invite a willing speaker who has experienced homelessness, or use a consented first-person video. Discuss how food insecurity, housing costs, disability, employment, family circumstances, and barriers to services can interact. Hear each person's account without treating a single story as everyone's experience.

**For local civic conversations:** During November council meetings or public gatherings, ask constructive, evidence-based questions about both immediate needs and stable housing. Different views are welcome. Check every statistic and clearly distinguish verified information from personal experience or proposed solutions.

**For November 14 Campout hosts:** Add an optional food-needs conversation or a confirmed, recipient-directed collection to your program. A safe sleep-out may create space for dialogue, but **one night outdoors cannot simulate or reproduce homelessness**. Never ask anyone to take unsafe risks, go without essentials, or reveal traumatic details.

## A suggested month-long sequence

- **November 1–7 ... Learn:** Share the month-long awareness observances, discuss youth and family housing stability, and choose an existing food-program recipient.
- **November 8–14 ... Invite:** Share stories and resources; acknowledge HETAC's optional November 10 Wear Green Day; on **Saturday, November 14**, hold the distributed, optional Chico Campout for Compassion.
- **November 15–21 ... Act:** Participate in National Hunger and Homelessness Awareness Week with recipient-approved food drives, volunteering, educational conversations, and sharing verified community resources. The November 17 City Council meeting is another opportunity to listen and ask questions.
- **November 22–30 ... Continue:** Deliver any confirmed collections, thank contributors and recipient organizations, share what was learned, and carry the conversation into December.

These are **suggested activities, not an announced schedule of public events**. Each group chooses what it can responsibly do.

## Working with news media and storytellers

National [HHWeek media materials](https://hhweek.org/) recommend a local contact list, a concise news release, and a short personal pitch. Our adaptation puts **consent and participant control first**. Never pressure anyone to share a personal history, promise an interview, make a person a prop, or share contact details without permission. Offer transportation or a speaker honorarium when appropriate. We should describe the Campout as independent, clearly separate from national organizers, and avoid recycled or unsourced statistics.

## Keep the evidence current

Some national toolkits contain national poverty, hunger, or homelessness figures from 2016–2022 and pandemic-era guidance. These are useful historical context **but are not current November 2026 statistics**. Avoid copying them onto flyers as if they describe Chico or the present day. Distinguish **youth homelessness**, **family housing instability**, **food insecurity**, and **point-in-time counts**, which use different definitions.
